# Reaction Mechanism Notation (Arrow-Pushing)

A reference glossary for the curved-arrow notation used to depict electron movement in organic and inorganic reaction mechanisms. German terms are included alongside the English/IUPAC-common terms since this notation is widely taught under these names in German-language chemistry curricula.

## Symbols

| Symbol | Name (DE) | Name (EN) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ⤴ (curved arrow, full double-barbed head) | Voller Pfeil | Full/curved arrow | **Two-electron shift.** A pair of electrons moves from the tail (a bond or lone pair) to the head (a new bond or lone pair). Used for heterolytic bond-breaking/forming steps, e.g. nucleophilic attack, proton transfer, most polar mechanism steps. |
| ⤵ (curved arrow, single-barbed "fish hook" head) | Fischhaken | Fish hook / half arrow | **One-electron shift.** A single electron moves from tail to head. Used for homolytic bond cleavage/formation — radical mechanisms, single-electron transfer (SET). |
| •• (pair of dots on an atom) | Punktpaar | Lone pair / dot pair | A **free (non-bonding) electron pair** on an atom, available to act as the tail of a full curved arrow (e.g. as a nucleophile or base). |
| ⊕ / ⊖ | Formalladung | Formal charge | Marks the **formal charge** on an atom in a structure — a bookkeeping charge from comparing an atom's valence electrons to its Lewis-structure electron count, not necessarily the true partial charge. |
| • (single dot on an atom) | Radikal | Radical | An **unpaired electron** on an atom or fragment, denoting a radical species (produced/consumed by fish-hook arrows). |
| ↔ (double-headed straight arrow) | Doppelpfeil | Resonance arrow | Connects **resonance structures (Mesomerie)** of the *same* species. It does **not** represent a reaction or an equilibrium — no atoms move and no reaction occurs; only the electron distribution is redrawn to show delocalization. Contrast with ⇌ (equilibrium arrows), which does denote a real, reversible reaction. |

## Usage notes

- Full arrows and fish hooks both start at an electron source (a bond or lone pair) and point to the destination (where the electron(s) end up) — the tail must start on the electrons, not on an atom.
- A homolytic mechanism step uses **two** fish hooks (one per electron) if a single bond breaks into two radicals; a heterolytic step uses **one** full arrow.
- Formal charges should be tracked before and after every arrow-pushing step: the sum of formal charges must be conserved across a mechanism step (charge is neither created nor destroyed, only redistributed unless an external electron/proton source or sink is explicit).
- Resonance structures linked by ↔ must have the same connectivity, same number of electrons, and are not separate species — never place a reaction condition (heat, reagent, catalyst) over a resonance arrow.
